# ADR 036 — Unique argument transfer into update callees (draft)

## Context

Round 13 insertion sort at 1,024/2,048 costs 151.94/1,021.74 ms versus core
2.71/2.99 ms. In `f(a,b)` the first `Replace` computes `a[b-1:a[b]]`, but a second
replacement still reads original `a[b-1]`; `last_uses` therefore keeps the first
replacement copying. Even a dead caller argument enters a normal allocating call
with caller roots retained: loop_storage's storage chains do not transfer unique
ownership into callees. The returned handle cannot inherit a whole-loop update
proof merely from its type. Counts across the 68 sources and B3 bodies will be
measured, distinguishing candidates from proved unique calls.

## Proposed decision (not accepted)

Derive a context-specific transfer only when caller argument is last-used, all
aliases/root descendants are dead or disjoint, and the callee's original-element
reads have been scheduled before mutation without moving any trap/effect. Preserve
immutable semantics for live aliases and returned aliases; evaluate all arguments
left-to-right before transfer. Allocation-return calls may not acquire a borrowing
summary. Track the original owner's quota/root handoff explicitly, including failure
exits, recursion, nested/lazy regions and sequence-bearing record children.

Forecast: removing whole-buffer copies changes suitable swaps from O(N) to O(1)
and insertion sort from copy-amplified superlinearity to O(N²) comparisons; not
O(N log N). Forecast 2,048 insertion sort improves materially but no defensible
absolute milliseconds until a transfer prototype exists. Linear builders should
retain geometric capacity; uncertain alias shapes keep copying. A separate goal
must preregister and measure this proof before migrating sort/concat/slice/updaters.

## Alternatives

User ownership annotations change language semantics. Borrow summaries cannot
represent allocating update ownership. Blind last-use transfer is unsound under
aliases and old reads. Whole-program inlining is neither a proof nor a bounded
solution to recursive/non-inlinable callees.

## Consequences

No implementation or accepted ownership change in Round 14. Existing conservative
copies remain. The audit will identify HIR operations and candidate frequency;
frequency is not evidence any candidate is safe to optimize.

## Status

Draft only, committed before Round 14 compiler changes. Requires independent design,
regression/fuzz/sanitizer evidence and measured transfer costs before acceptance.
